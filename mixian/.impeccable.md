## Design Context

Students use the customer UI to order rice noodles to their dorms. Store staff and riders use separate, task-focused workspaces. Interfaces should feel direct, practical and local rather than like a campaign or demo. The customer interface uses white surfaces on a light warm-gray canvas, food photography and one chili-red action color, with line icons sourced through Morphicons Studio.

- Show price, availability and the next action before secondary details.
- Use visible selection states, familiar controls and descriptive action labels.
- Keep the roles separate; no QA or prototype controls in product navigation.
- Show order progress through explicit steps and keep payment, verification and refund distinct.

### Design preferences from prototype reviews

- Use a light warm-gray canvas and pure-white rounded surfaces. Separate sections with space and subtle shadows; reserve borders for states and row dividers.
- Use chili red for primary actions, prices and selected states. Use solid icons for the active bottom tab and outline icons for inactive tabs. Keep the bar flush with the viewport and respect the safe area.
- Make order cards tappable throughout, with a distinct order number, quieter timestamp and small directional cue. Keep one obvious next action on each detail screen.
- Hide duplicated brand bars, instructions and navigation. Use short labels, keep supporting metadata secondary and show exceptions where they change the next action.
- Display saved values before editing. Use short bottom sheets for edits and confirmations; allow tapping outside to dismiss and preserve visible focus targets.
- Merchant screens prioritize payment checks and exceptions. Rider screens prioritize destination and delivery window, then the next step.
